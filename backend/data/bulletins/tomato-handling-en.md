Tomatoes are highly perishable. At ordinary room temperature, ripe tomatoes last only a few days before they soften and lose market value. Holding ripe tomatoes in the hope of a better price is risky; small price gains are usually wiped out by spoilage and weight loss.

Harvest at the right stage for the distance to market. For nearby markets, tomatoes can be picked at the pink to light red stage. For distant markets, pick at the breaker or turning stage so that the fruit ripens on the way.

Harvest in the cool hours of the morning or evening and keep picked fruit in the shade. Heat in the field speeds up softening and rotting.

Pack in clean, ventilated plastic crates rather than deep sacks or baskets. Crates reduce bruising and compression damage during transport, and undamaged fruit gets a better price.

Sort out cracked, diseased and over-ripe fruit before packing. Damaged fruit spoils quickly and spreads rot to sound fruit in the same crate.
