A higher price at a distant market is only better if it is still higher after costs. Before choosing a market, subtract transport (vehicle hire per quintal), loading and unloading charges, and market fees or commission from the price you expect to get.

The modal price is the price at which most produce was traded that day. Your own price depends on quality and grade, so treat the modal price as a guide and the min to max range as the likely spread.

Prices reported for a market can be a day or more old. When the latest report is several days old, prices may have moved, and a decision based on it is less reliable.

Heavy arrivals usually push prices down for a few days. Watch arrival volumes along with prices when deciding when to sell.
