Cure potatoes before storage. After digging, keep the tubers in a shaded, airy place for 10 to 15 days so that the skin hardens and small wounds heal. Cured tubers lose less weight and rot less in storage.

Sort before storing. Remove cut, bruised, rotten, green and very small tubers. Damaged tubers rot first and spread rot to sound tubers around them.

Cold storage keeps table potatoes for several months with little loss. Rented cold storage is charged per quintal for the season, so include that rent when deciding whether to hold potatoes for a better price. Potatoes stored at home in heaps or pits lose much more weight and rot faster, especially in hot months.

Keep potatoes in the dark. Light turns the skin green, and green potatoes are bitter and get a lower price.

Before taking potatoes out of cold storage for sale, let them warm up slowly for a day or two. This reduces sweating and rot after removal.
