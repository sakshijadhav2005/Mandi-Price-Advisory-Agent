Cure onions before storing them. After lifting, leave the bulbs in the field in small heaps with the leaves covering the bulbs for 3 to 5 days, then cut the tops leaving about 2 to 2.5 cm of neck. Properly cured bulbs have a dry, tight neck and store far better than uncured bulbs.

Only rabi (late season) onions are suitable for long storage. Kharif onions have a short dormancy and start sprouting and rotting within weeks, so they should normally be sold soon after harvest.

Store onions in a well ventilated structure with a raised, slatted floor so that air moves through the heap from below. Keep the heap no higher than about 1.5 metres. Bottom ventilation reduces heat and moisture build up, which are the main causes of rotting.

Expect storage losses. Even in a good ventilated store, onions lose weight through moisture loss, sprouting and rotting. Losses of around 1 percent per week in the first months are common, and losses rise in the hot, humid months. Include this loss in any decision to hold onions for a better price.

Grade before storage and remove bulbs that are damaged, doubled, thick-necked or already sprouting. One rotting bulb can spread rot to its neighbours. Check the store every week and remove spoiled bulbs.
